02 · How I work

Fast, and done properly.

Tooling has changed how much a small team can get through in a week. The hard part is keeping the quality bar, and everyone’s understanding of the codebase, intact at that pace.

  1. 01

    Every change gets a spec, tests and a review.

    I learned in banking that untested code is a liability. That applies whether a person or an agent wrote the first draft.

  2. 02

    I review and own everything that merges.

    I run several workstreams in parallel with AI coding agents. Each one gets a scoped ticket, its own branch and automated checks, and nothing lands without my review. That is what lets a small team cover a lot of ground while keeping the codebase coherent.

  3. 03

    You decide whether AI tools are used at all.

    Your code is yours. I only use tools you have approved, on terms that don’t train on it. If your policy is no AI, I work without it.

  4. 04

    Tickets and pull requests are written for people.

    A plain-English summary at the top, technical detail below. Everyone on the team can see what is changing and why.

Yes. AI coding agents handle the things they’re good at: scaffolding, tests, refactors and working several tickets in parallel. I review everything before it merges, and architecture, UX and judgement calls stay with me. If your organisation doesn’t allow AI tools, I’ll work without them. I was shipping production C# for over a decade before they existed.
